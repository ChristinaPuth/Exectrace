## 6. Decisions made during M3 that the spec did not fix

Please confirm or change these in manual test MT-3.9.

1. **Extraction is part of `leadership collect`**, in the same run and under
   the same limits. `--no-extract` stops after fetching.
2. **The quote must be near the person's name** (250 characters). New
   requirement R156.
3. **A date must be written in full, next to the quote**, to be stored. New
   requirement R157. Month-only dates are lost, not rounded. Only
   appointments and departures carry a date.
3a. **An end date is only a date a source states** (R162). A role can be
   `former` with no end date; the date of the reporting source is kept
   apart in `end_reported_on`.
3b. **Unverified people are counted apart from current leaders** (R163), in
   the run report, the counters and the second-search trigger.
4. **A title in a unit of the company** keeps the unit's name and is not a
   company-wide single-holder role. New requirement R158.
5. **The name is stored as each source wrote it**, and
   `tools/check_evidence.py` can re-check every claim. New requirement R159.
6. **R153 is implemented by a page-kind judgment from the model**, applied
   by code: a third-party page judged a profile or directory drops to tier
   4. It can only lower a third-party source. In the final data no page was
   lowered, because the M2 exclusions had already removed such pages.
7. **Long documents are read in part**: at most 3 pieces of 4,500 characters,
   chosen by leadership wording.
8. **Presidents and general counsels are C-level.** Directors, board members
   and founders without an office are stored as `other`, outside the scope.
9. **Location and bio are not extracted in M3** beyond a location the page
   states outright. Enrichment is M4.
10. **Two version stamps.** The model answer is cached by prompt, schema and
    model. A change to the checks or rules re-processes documents from that
    cache with no model call.
11. **A run with no LLM key ends `insufficient_data`**, saying no people
    could be extracted.
12. **Four database columns were added**: `sources.extraction_version`,
    `sources.page_kind`, `people.possible_duplicate_of`, and
    `claims.person_as_written`. Older databases get them on opening.

## 7. Known limitations and what M3 does not cover

### Limitations of what was built

- **Extraction recall: a stated date can be missed (Jason Warnick).** The
  proxy statement says he "ceased to serve as Chief Financial Officer
  effective February 6, 2026". The passage was sent to the model, but the
  model did not return that sentence, so no claim carries the date. The
  database therefore shows his CFO role as `former` with **no end date**
  and "reported by a source dated 2026-02-10". This is a recall limitation
  of extraction, not a rule decision: the date exists in a source we hold
  and we did not capture it. Nothing in M3 detects such a miss. Recovering
  explicitly stated dates is M4 requirement R164.
- **The 250-character evidence window.** The quote must have the person's
  surname, and any effective date, in it or within 250 characters. This is
  a fixed distance, not an understanding of the text. It can wrongly accept
  a quote that sits near a different person with the same surname, or a
  date nearby that belongs to another event. It can wrongly refuse a
  correct claim when a long paragraph puts the name or the date more than
  250 characters from the quote, or when a later sentence uses only "he" or
  "she". Refusals are logged; wrong acceptances are not detectable by the
  tool.
- **Long documents are read in part.** At most three pieces of 4,500
  characters are sent to the model, chosen by counting leadership wording.
  Robinhood's proxy statement has 88 pieces and its annual report 174, so
  about 3% and 2% of them are read. A relevant passage that scores low is
  never seen. The choice is by keywords, so it can prefer a signature page
  to a paragraph that states a departure. Which pieces were read is not
  recorded per claim.
- **The C-level convention.** "C-level" here means: any "Chief ... Officer"
  title or its abbreviation, any President who is not a Vice President, and
  a General Counsel. That is a convention I chose, not a standard. It makes
  "Chief Accounting Officer" and "Chief Brokerage Officer" C-level, and also
  the chief officer of a single unit ("Chief Compliance Officer, Robinhood
  Financial"), who is C-level by title but not a company-wide officer. It
  excludes "Chief of Staff", founders without an office, chairs and board
  members, and "Managing Director". Counts of C-level leaders, and the M4
  gold-set recall for C-level, depend on this convention.

### Not covered in M3

- **Verification of unverified and conflicting roles** (M4). Seven
  Robinhood and two Campfire leader roles wait for it.
- **Location and biography** (M4), and the export of fixtures (M4).
- **Gold-set precision and recall of the whole pipeline** (M4). M3 measures
  five pages only.
- **Robinhood's VPs and Heads.** None was found: the sources read are
  filings and appointment announcements, which name officers.
- **Near-duplicate people.** Two are flagged and kept separate ("Dan
  Gallagher" and "Vlad Tenev", each holding only a board or founder
  mention). Nothing merges them automatically.
- **The invented-URL case of link selection** (M2) is still untested.
- **Groq's daily request limit** is still not counted locally.
